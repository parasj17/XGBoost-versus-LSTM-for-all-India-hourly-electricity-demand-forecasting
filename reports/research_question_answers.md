# Answers to the research questions (written 2026-10-15, after the single test run)

> **Read `docs/limitations.md` first.** The main limits: one test year; validation numbers are optimistic; tuning budgets differed (an equal-budget LSTM was added later, see `reports/lstm_stage3.md`); the test period was reused for declared secondary analyses; some inputs are unverified.

Evidence base: test period 2024-07-01 to 2025-06-24 (one run, 8,611 valid hours per horizon), validation 2024-H1, rolling-origin folds on 2023. Every number below is in `reports/test_results.md`, `reports/ablation_paired.md`, `reports/uncertainty.md`, `reports/rolling_origin.md`, `reports/hour_of_day.md`, `reports/robustness.md` or `reports/validation_summary.md`. All statements are about all-India hourly demand from this dataset, one test year, one hardware setup, and the tuning budgets stated in the protocol (XGBoost 90 trials per horizon, LSTM 12 + 12). "Not distinguishable" means the 95% block-bootstrap interval includes zero. Nothing here is a causal claim.

## Central question: how do XGBoost and LSTM compare?
Test MAPE (1 / 6 / 24 / 48 h): XGBoost 0.57 / 1.43 / 1.95 / 2.70 %; ridge 0.54 / 1.60 / 2.21 / 2.90 %; LSTM full features 0.84 / 1.65 / 1.96 / 2.79 %; best naive 2.25 / 2.82 / 2.82 / 3.64 %. XGBoost has the lowest error from 6 h on; at 1 h ridge and XGBoost are not distinguishable. The LSTM was never ahead of XGBoost on the point estimate in the test run or in the rolling-origin folds, was clearly worse at 1 h and 6 h, and was not distinguishable from XGBoost at 24 h and 48 h on the test year. A learning-curve diagnostic gave no sign that more training data alone would change this (the LSTM's error was flat in training size), and giving the LSTM an equal tuning budget (90 trials per horizon, as for XGBoost; `reports/lstm_stage3_test.md`) did not change the comparison: test MAE 1,549 / 3,151 / 3,776 / 5,418 MW against 1,111 / 2,747 / 3,728 / 5,158 for XGBoost; clearly worse at 1 h and 6 h, not distinguishable at 24 h and 48 h. Compute: XGBoost tuning cost 5-11 s per trial and its refits took 5-62 s (three seeds); LSTM tuning cost about 60-66 s per trial and its refits 35-950 s, i.e. roughly 4 to 45 times more per refit depending on horizon and variant, on a 4-thread laptop CPU.

## 1. Does weather improve forecasts beyond history and calendar?
- **Past observed weather: no measurable effect on the test period.** XGBoost S2 -> S3 (adding national and regional weather up to the forecast origin): +11 MW (CI -3 to +22) at 1 h, +11 (-39 to +66) at 6 h, +8 (-57 to +80) at 24 h, -18 (-101 to +65) at 48 h. On validation it had looked helpful at 24-48 h (about -2 to -3%); that did not carry over to the test year.
- **The LSTM was hurt by the full input set** (weather, solar/wind, holidays): +116 MW (CI +75 to +153) at 1 h and +189 (+48 to +308) at 6 h; neutral at 24-48 h.
- **Future weather would help at long horizons.** With the actual weather at the target hour (an oracle, standing in for a perfect forecast; ERA5 reanalysis): -219 MW (-308 to -151; -5.9%) at 24 h and -594 (-854 to -339; -11.5%) at 48 h; nothing at 1 h or 6 h. A real forecast would be worse than this bound (a one-month spot check, May 2023, of archived GFS forecasts at Delhi showed a +3 to +4 C bias against ERA5 and 4-5 C mean errors for day-1 and day-2 forecasts).
- **Archived forecast temperature (scenario C, an exploratory extension declared in advance and run once on the test period) does help at the longer horizons.** Adding the archived GFS day-1/day-2 temperature forecast for the target hour to the history+calendar+holiday model: -165 MW (CI -235 to -107; -4.5%) at 24 h and -375 MW (-541 to -187; -7.3%) at 48 h, -13 (-35 to +13) at 6 h, and slightly worse at 1 h (+10, +4 to +17). In size that is roughly 75% (24 h) and 63% (48 h) of the gain of the oracle comparison above (-219 and -594 MW), although the two comparisons start from slightly different reference models (S2 and S4). Limits: only temperature could be used (the archive has no humidity, wind, cloud, radiation or precipitation before 2024), the archive's issue times were not verified so the forecasts may be slightly optimistic, 10% of the validation hours had no forecast, and this does not change the frozen headline model (`reports/extensions_xgb_test.md`).
- **A population-weighted state-capital weather series (task 9.4, exploratory, validation first and one test run; `reports/statepop_weather_test.md`) did not change the picture.** It correlates 0.9965 with the 12-city temperature series. On validation no difference had an interval away from zero. On test, XGBoost with the state series was 1-2% better at 24 h and 48 h (S4: -54 MW, CI -103 to -20, and -86 MW, CI -155 to -0), but the same comparison had the opposite sign on validation (S4 at 48 h: +84 MW), and ridge was not helped (+1, +25, +4, -5 MW), so the evidence for any robust effect is weak. The series uses capital-city points, not true gridded state means.
- Plausible reading, not tested: recent demand history already contains the effect of recent weather, and one national average of 12 cities may blur regional heat. The calendar group, in contrast, is worth 10-12% at 6-48 h (S0 -> S1, intervals well away from zero).

## 2. Do Indian holiday/festival indicators help beyond ordinary calendar features?
- **Small gain, not distinguishable from zero on one year.** XGBoost S1 -> S2: +1 MW (-8 to +10) at 1 h; -32 (-109 to +32; -1.2%) at 6 h; -78 (-208 to +37; -2.1%) at 24 h; -56 (-270 to +113; -1.1%) at 48 h. The sign is consistent for 6-48 h, the intervals include zero.
- Error is clearly elevated around holidays and festivals: within one day of a major festival XGBoost's MAE was 3,515 / 6,214 / 6,524 MW at 6 / 24 / 48 h against 2,747 / 3,728 / 5,158 overall (1.3-1.7 times; 360 hours, about 15 days, so a small sample; not elevated at 1 h). At 48 h ridge and the LSTMs were worse still (9,000-10,100 MW, 1.7-1.9 times).
- The main feature set contains only central gazetted holidays and an analyst-defined list of major festivals. **Regional (state-level) holidays, tried as an exploratory extension, added nothing:** population-weighted share of states with a holiday, from the `holidays` package, S2 -> S7 on the test period: +5 MW (+2 to +7) at 1 h, +17 (-7 to +37) at 6 h, -23 (-73 to +18) at 24 h, +13 (-43 to +73) at 48 h. The source is incomplete (it misses multi-day regional festival windows such as Durga Puja) and its state calendars were not checked against state gazettes, so this is weak evidence about regional festivals as such (`reports/regional_holidays.md`, `reports/extensions_xgb_test.md`).

## 3. How does the relative performance change with the horizon?
- **1 h:** ridge and XGBoost are both about 75% better than persistence and are not distinguishable (XGBoost minus ridge +62 MW, CI -14 to +202); ridge was at least as good in all three rolling folds and on validation. The LSTM is 400-530 MW worse. Short-horizon demand is almost autoregressive; XGBoost's feature importance was dominated by the last hour.
- **6 h:** XGBoost best; it beats ridge on test (-335 MW, -416 to -237) and validation, but in the rolling folds the ranking against ridge flipped (fold 1: ridge better by 224 MW, fold 2: XGBoost better by 101 MW). Treat the 6 h XGBoost advantage as less certain than the 24 h one. The LSTM is behind (+423 and +234 MW).
- **24 h:** XGBoost beats ridge in every fold and on test (-486 MW, -615 to -354); the LSTMs match XGBoost within noise (+14 MW, -110 to +105 for the full-feature LSTM; +24, -81 to +154 for history+calendar) and also beat ridge.
- **48 h:** XGBoost beats ridge (-391 MW, -647 to -153); the LSTMs are not distinguishable from XGBoost on test (+167, -101 to +429) but were behind it in the rolling folds (tie in fold 1, +426 MW in fold 2).
- **72 h and 168 h (exploratory extension 9.5; `reports/long_horizons_test.md`; validation tuning, one test run).** 72 h: test MAE ridge 6,244, XGBoost 6,018, LSTM 6,200 MW against 7,676 for the best naive baseline (skill about 19-22%); XGBoost minus ridge -225 MW (CI -506 to +46) and LSTM minus XGBoost +182 MW (CI -201 to +552), i.e. not distinguishable. 168 h: ridge 7,743, XGBoost 8,173, LSTM 8,171 MW against 8,476 for naive (skill 8.7%, 3.6% and 3.6%); ridge is clearly better than XGBoost (+431 MW for XGBoost, CI +169 to +766) and than the LSTM (+428, CI +155 to +754), and XGBoost and the LSTM are tied. On validation the selected XGBoost and LSTM at 168 h stopped improving after only 2 trees and 1 epoch, i.e. they did not generalise from 2021-2023 to 2024. A week ahead the simple model wins and the learned models add little over last week's value. Tuning budgets were smaller than for the main horizons (XGBoost 30 trials, LSTM 12 trials).
- **Feature-group ablation at 72 h and 168 h (extension; `reports/long_ablation_test.md`; trees fixed on validation, one test run).** 72 h: calendar features help (-423 MW, CI -652 to -156; -6.5%); holidays (-73, CI -337 to +139), past weather (-47, -151 to +63) and past solar/wind (+50, -107 to +186) show nothing; oracle weather at the target hour helps strongly (-697, -1,026 to -413; -11.6%), the same pattern as at 24 h and 48 h. 168 h: the early-stopped XGBoost models have only 1 to 15 trees (nearly constant predictions), so the individual intervals that exclude zero (holidays +407, past weather -334, past solar/wind +224) mostly reflect small differences between near-constant models and should not be interpreted; ridge (7,743 MW) beats every one of these XGBoost sets (best 7,877).
- **Rolling-origin folds and learning curve at 72 h and 168 h (extensions; `reports/rolling_origin_long.md`, `reports/long_learning_curve.md`).** Rolling origin (two folds on 2023, frozen long-horizon configurations, MAE in MW): at 72 h ridge beat XGBoost in 2023-H1 (6,361 vs 6,633) and XGBoost beat ridge in 2023-H2 (5,867 vs 6,487), so the ranking flips between folds as it does at 6 h; at 168 h ridge and XGBoost are tied in both folds (differences of 15 and 26 MW) and all models are only 1-4% better than naive. The LSTM is behind XGBoost in all four fold-horizon cells (by 56 to 354 MW). Learning curve (validation, MAE in MW; LSTM / XGBoost ratio): 72 h 12 / 18 / 28 months of training data 5,550 / 5,302 / 4,904 for XGBoost and 6,159 / 6,265 / 5,597 for the LSTM (ratios 1.11, 1.18, 1.14); 168 h 6,409 / 6,414 / 6,272 and 6,398 / 6,500 / 6,549 (ratios 1.00, 1.01, 1.04). As at the main horizons, more training data does not close the gap between the LSTM and XGBoost within this range.
- **Cross-validated tuning at 72 h and 168 h (extension; `reports/long_cv_tuning.md`, `reports/long_cv_tuning_test.md`).** To test whether early stopping on the single validation half-year caused the poor 168 h result, ridge, XGBoost S4 and the LSTM were re-tuned by expanding-window cross-validation inside the training period (three half-year folds of 2022-2023), leaving the validation period as a true hold-out. Validation hold-out (MAE in MW; best naive 6,828 at 72 h and 6,432 at 168 h): 72 h ridge 5,266, XGBoost 5,359, LSTM 5,822; 168 h ridge 6,062, XGBoost 6,998, LSTM 6,967 (cross-validation chose 41 trees and 1 epoch instead of 2 trees and 1 epoch, and the hold-out error got worse; XGBoost and the LSTM were then worse than naive). On the test period the CV-tuned models were not distinguishable from the validation-tuned originals (XGBoost +173 MW at 72 h, CI -45 to +391, and -126 MW at 168 h, CI -546 to +351; LSTM +101 and -233 MW, intervals include zero), and ridge was still the best model at 168 h (7,728 against 8,048 for XGBoost and 7,938 for the LSTM, differences not distinguishable). The weak 168 h result for the learned models is therefore not an artefact of how the training length was chosen; the 2021-2023 patterns appear not to transfer to 2024.
- **Equal tuning budget for the LSTM at 72 h and 168 h (extension; `reports/long_lstm_tuning.md`, `reports/long_lstm_tuning_test.md`).** 30 random trials per horizon (XGBoost's budget there) did not improve on the original configuration at 72 h (the original was kept; identical test MAE 6,200 MW) and gave a slightly worse test result at 168 h (8,260 against 8,171 MW, difference +90 MW, CI +10 to +168). Against XGBoost the LSTM stays not distinguishable (+182 MW, CI -201 to +552 at 72 h; +87 MW, CI -75 to +233 at 168 h).
- **Forecast temperature and regional holidays at 72 h and 168 h (extension; `reports/long_extensions_xgb_test.md`; archived GFS temperature forecasts with leads of 3 and 7 days; validation first, one test run).** Forecast temperature helps clearly: test MAE 5,604 against 6,016 MW at 72 h (-412 MW, CI -624 to -186; -6.8%) and 7,642 against 8,284 MW at 168 h (-642 MW, CI -881 to -376; -7.7%); at 168 h that XGBoost model (7,642) is below the frozen ridge (7,743), a gap that was not tested. Regional holidays added nothing (+19 MW, CI -36 to +90 at 72 h; -85 MW, CI -204 to +22 at 168 h). The same caveats as at the main horizons apply (11-14% of validation hours without an archived forecast; issue times not verified).
- **Prediction intervals at 72 h and 168 h (extension; `reports/long_uncertainty.md`; calibrated on validation, one test run).** Coverage of the nominal 90% intervals is poor and worse than at the main horizons: at 72 h split conformal covers 81.7-85.0% and rolling conformal 86.9-87.7%; at 168 h split conformal covers 78.2-79.0% and rolling conformal 83.6-84.2%. The raw quantile models were closer at 168 h (XGBoost and LSTM both 86.2%) but the conformal correction (CQR) lowered their coverage to 82.4% and 81.5%, because the validation period was easier than the test year. By interval score the XGBoost quantile regression was best at both horizons (CQR 35,251 MW at 72 h, raw 45,080 MW at 168 h). Interval widths are 20.1-25.8 GW at 72 h (10-13% of mean test demand) and 24.4-32.2 GW at 168 h (13-17%). At 72 h the shortfall concentrates in the monsoon (75%) and pre-monsoon (78%) seasons for XGBoost with split conformal; at 168 h in the same seasons (72% and 75%).
- Breakdowns and feature importance at 72 h and 168 h are in `reports/long_breakdowns.md` and `reports/long_importance.md` (descriptive; the 168 h XGBoost has only 2 trees).
- Test skill against the best naive baseline for XGBoost: about 74% at 1 h, 49% at 6 h, 31% at 24 h, 26% at 48 h; the skill falls with the horizon for every model.

## 4. Can prediction intervals give useful uncertainty estimates at acceptable cost?
90% intervals, calibrated on validation only (`reports/uncertainty.md`).
- **Coverage on the test year (XGBoost S4):** 89.5-91.1% at 1 h, 88.8-89.5% at 6 h, 86.6-89.1% at 24 h, 84.5-88.1% at 48 h across the split-conformal and rolling-conformal variants. Short horizons come close to nominal; long horizons under-cover, as expected when demand has grown and the test year is harder than validation. Rolling conformal (last 720 h of errors known at the forecast origin) removes much of the shortfall (48 h: 88.1% versus 84.8%) at the price of wider intervals (21.0 versus 18.8 GW). The shortfall concentrates in the monsoon and pre-monsoon seasons and in the afternoon hours (48 h split conformal: monsoon 78%, 12-17 h 81%).
- **Width:** mean 4.7 GW at 1 h up to 18.8-21.0 GW at 48 h (2.4% to 9.7% of mean test demand for split conformal, up to 10.9% for the rolling variant). Raw XGBoost quantile regression under-covered at 24 h (81.6%) and 48 h (86.2%); after conformal correction (CQR) coverage was 86.9% at 24 h and 88.6% at 48 h; by interval score the conformalised quantile model was best at 24 h and 48 h, ridge at 1 h, and XGBoost with conformal intervals at 6 h.
- **LSTM quantile head (5%/95%, three seeds averaged):** raw coverage 84.8 / 89.4 / 86.8 / 87.1% at 1 / 6 / 24 / 48 h; after conformal correction 88.1 / 90.3 / 87.8 / 85.4% (at 48 h the correction lowered coverage because the validation year was easier than the test year). By interval score the LSTM quantile head was the best method at 24 h (24,720 MW against 25,341 for the conformalised XGBoost quantile model) and worse than XGBoost at 6 h and 48 h and than ridge at 1 h. Training the LSTM quantile models took about 16 minutes for three seeds at 1 h against 32 seconds for one XGBoost quantile model.
- **Cost:** conformal intervals cost essentially nothing beyond the point model (a quantile of stored residuals); XGBoost quantile regression needed one extra model per horizon (fit 9-32 s, 0.08-0.24 s to predict 8.6 thousand hours). The LSTM received conformal intervals and a quantile head (MC dropout was not run). Its conformal intervals were wider than XGBoost's at 1 h and 6 h.
- Limits: validation was also used for early stopping of the quantile models, one test year, intervals are marginal (not guaranteed per season or hour).

## 5. Does solar/wind generation information help?
- **Not on the test period.** XGBoost S3 -> S4 (past solar and wind added): +11 MW (+6 to +17) at 1 h (slightly worse), +30 (-7 to +75) at 6 h, +15 (-37 to +78) at 24 h, +19 (-99 to +127) at 48 h. On validation it had looked slightly helpful at 24-48 h (about -2%). The LSTM with these inputs was no better at 24-48 h and worse at 1-6 h.
- **Likely reason (a hypothesis, supported by data shift):** installed solar capacity kept growing; 15.4% of test hours exceed the training maximum of solar generation (8.9% of validation hours) and mean test solar is 59% higher than in training. Wind stayed inside its training range. Features expressed as a share of installed capacity were not tried and might behave differently.
- Data caveats: solar night-time offsets were set to zero using radiation, daytime offsets cannot be corrected, and only past generation (up to the forecast origin) is allowed as input, which is a different question from using forecast generation.

## Walk-forward evaluation with quarterly retraining (exploratory; `reports/walk_forward.md`)
Models retrained every quarter on all earlier data with the frozen hyper-parameters, scored on six quarterly windows from 2024-Q1 to 2025-Q2. **The first two windows lie in the validation period (not independent); the other four overlap the test period.** Mean MAE over the four windows from 2024-Q3 (MW) and the number of windows (of six) won:

| Horizon | ridge | XGBoost | LSTM L4 | LSTM equal budget | XGBoost better than ridge | LSTM L4 better than XGBoost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 h | 1,041 | 1,089 | 1,288 | 1,344 | 2 of 6 | 1 of 6 |
| 6 h | 3,100 | 2,673 | 2,999 | 3,139 | 6 of 6 | 0 of 6 |
| 24 h | 4,227 | 3,688 | 3,754 | 3,758 | 6 of 6 | 1 of 6 |
| 48 h | 5,565 | 5,036 | 5,300 | 5,345 | 5 of 6 | 1 of 6 |

With regular retraining XGBoost beat ridge in every window at 6 h and 24 h and in five of six at 48 h; at 1 h ridge was ahead in four of six windows (mean difference +47 MW in ridge's favour over the four windows from 2024-Q3), so the 1 h question stays unsettled. The LSTM was ahead of XGBoost in at most one window per horizon; at 24 h the two are close on average (+66 MW for the LSTM) and the equal-budget LSTM is no better than the original. These windows are not independent evaluations of an untouched period, and there are only six of them.

### Walk-forward at 72 h and 168 h (extension; `reports/walk_forward_long.md`)
The same quarterly retraining at the long horizons, with the long-horizon configurations and the same flags (the first two windows are in the validation period). Mean MAE over the four windows from 2024-Q3 (MW): 72 h: naive 7,539, ridge 6,239, XGBoost 5,913, LSTM 6,089 (XGBoost better than ridge in 5 of 6 windows; the LSTM better than XGBoost in 2 of 6); 168 h: naive 8,527, ridge 7,777, XGBoost 8,246, LSTM 8,198 (XGBoost better than ridge in 0 of 6 windows; the LSTM better than XGBoost in 3 of 6). A week ahead ridge was better than XGBoost in every window and the learned models were only about 3-4% better than the naive baseline, in line with the validation and test results at 168 h.

## Daily multi-year walk-forward on the full Zenodo series, 2017-2024 (exploratory; `reports/daily_multiyear.md`)
The Zenodo national daily energy (2013 to 2024-04) is daily, so it cannot answer the hourly questions, but it allows eight test years with an expanding training window (hyper-parameters chosen once on 2021-2022 and frozen; an LSTM was added). Mean MAE over the folds (MU) and wins (of 8 folds):

| | naive | ridge | XGBoost | LSTM | XGBoost better than ridge | LSTM better than XGBoost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 day, all 8 folds | 138.7 | 50.7 | 52.0 | 51.5 | 3 | 6 |
| 1 day, 5 clean folds | 132.4 | 48.8 | 48.8 | 49.7 | | |
| 7 days, all 8 folds | 137.9 | 129.2 | 125.2 | 127.7 | 6 | 3 |
| 7 days, 5 clean folds | 133.1 | 118.0 | 114.8 | 119.8 | | |

"Clean" excludes 2021 and 2022 (the tuning period) and 2020 (COVID). At daily resolution and with up to 11 years of training data no model is consistently best: at 1 day the three learned models are within about 4% of each other and the winner changes from year to year (ridge 2017, 2019 and 2020; LSTM 2018, 2021, 2022 and 2023; XGBoost 2024); at 7 days XGBoost has a small edge (about 3% over ridge and 5% over the LSTM on the clean folds). In the COVID year ridge was clearly best at 1 day (57.0 MU against 69.7 for XGBoost and 66.8 for the LSTM), and at 7 days ridge (169.8) and XGBoost (163.2) were worse than the naive baseline (155.2) while the LSTM (155.8) only matched it. The LSTM was behind XGBoost in all three folds with at most 1,600 training days at 7 days, which is weak evidence of data hunger at small sizes, but it was not consistently behind at 1 day (ahead in 2018 and 2019). Eight test years give a better view of year-to-year variation than the single hourly test year, but they are daily, national, two horizons only, with hyper-parameters tuned on two of the years.

## Extensions that were run (exploratory, declared in advance, secondary to the frozen headline results)
- **Scenario C (forecast temperature) and regional holidays:** see questions 1 and 2 above.
- **LSTM uncertainty:** quantile head, see question 4.
- **Daily multi-year walk-forward:** see the section above.
- **Regional daily study** (Zenodo state-level daily energy, 2013 to 2024-04, test 2023-01-01 to 2024-04-28, evaluated once; `reports/regional_daily_study_test.md`): forecasting by region and summing did **not** beat forecasting the national total directly. Test MAE (MU), 1 day: naive 182.4, ridge 58.1, XGBoost direct 61.0, XGBoost bottom-up 72.0 (bottom-up minus direct +11.1, CI +1.9 to +17.9); 7 days: naive 181.0, ridge 164.6, direct 164.0, bottom-up 168.0 (bottom-up minus direct +4.1, CI -12.0 to +14.7). Bottom-up had looked better on validation (45.3 vs 49.9 MU at 1 day), which did not carry over. At 1 day ridge and XGBoost direct are not distinguishable (+2.8 MU, CI -3.3 to +10.3). Regional models reused the hyper-parameters tuned for the national series and there was a single 16-month test window, so read this as 'not supported', not as disproved.
- **Novelty check:** see `docs/novelty_assessment.md` (preliminary web search; no study found that combines the elements, which is not proof that none exists).

## Limitations to state in any write-up
- One test year; one split; two rolling folds of half a year on 2023 plus the main validation split; rolling-origin results use the hyper-parameters frozen on the main split.
- Tuning budgets: the frozen comparison had XGBoost at about 90 trials per horizon and the LSTM at 12 + 12; an equal-budget LSTM (90 trials, 10-epoch trials, 70% of the 1 h trials pruned early) was added afterwards and did not change the conclusions. Statements about "LSTM versus XGBoost" still apply to this setup (one small network, a 168-hour window, three seeds, one CPU).
- Validation was used for early stopping, tuning and calibration, so validation numbers are optimistic; the test numbers are the honest ones.
- The cleaning rules were chosen looking at the full series (including the later years) from physical plausibility, not from forecast performance.
- Weather is ERA5 reanalysis, not an operational forecast. Scenario C (archived forecasts) was run only for temperature, because the archive has no other variable before 2024, and its issue times were not verified.
- Demand growth makes about 29% of test hours exceed the training 95th percentile, so "peak hours" is not a rare-event subset.
