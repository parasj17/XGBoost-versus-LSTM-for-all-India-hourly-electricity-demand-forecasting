Pearson r between hourly national temperature and demand over the training period: 0.648; between temperature and the de-levelled ratio (afternoon): -0.198.
Caution: these numbers are confounded by season. Cool afternoons occur in winter, when the afternoon-to-daily-mean ratio is structurally larger, so the left side of the curve is not a pure heating effect. The curve is clearly non-linear (lowest around 30 deg C, rising on both sides), so a single linear temperature term would be a poor representation; the model should receive temperature together with hour and season and be allowed to learn the shape.

## Evidence for the trend decision (D-07)
Seasonal-naive-168 (same hour last week) in the training period: MAE 7.30 GW; MAPE 4.26%.
Week-over-week change of the weekly mean: median +0.54%, std 4.04% - this shows how much the level moves from week to week (mostly seasonal), on top of the year-to-year growth reported above.
Std of the ratio y(t)/y(t-168): 0.058.
Interpretation: demand is non-stationary in level. A tree model fitted on absolute MW cannot predict levels above the training maximum. This analysis does not examine later years; the earlier all-years data audit (not part of this analysis) indicated that demand keeps rising, so the issue is expected to matter and must be confirmed on validation. A target expressed relative to a lagged anchor removes most of the level drift. Recommendation (not yet frozen): primary candidate = ratio to the seasonal anchor; keep absolute MW as the comparison variant. The final choice must be made on VALIDATION results in Phase 5.
